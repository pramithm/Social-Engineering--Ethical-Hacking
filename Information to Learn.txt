### 1. The Real-Life Story: Why Does This Project Exist?

Imagine you run a company with 500 employees. 

You spent \$1,000,000 buying the world’s best firewalls, antivirus, and security software. Nobody can hack through your network.

**Then, on Monday morning, a hacker sends a simple email to your Finance accountant:**
> *"Hi, this is the CEO. I'm in an urgent meeting. Click this link and verify your password right now or payroll will be delayed."*

What does the employee do? 
They panic. They see the CEO’s name. They click the link, and they type their corporate password into a fake login screen.

**Game over. The entire company is hacked.** 
The hacker didn't break the firewall. They hacked the **human**. That is called **Social Engineering** (Phishing).

### The Big Problem Companies Face:
1. Companies don’t know **which departments are the weakest link** (Is it Finance? HR? Marketing?).
2. When employees get tricked, companies usually punish or embarrass them, so employees hide their mistakes instead of reporting them.
3. If the IT department puts a warning banner like *"CAUTION: External Email"*, **does it actually work?** Or do employees just ignore it?

---

### 2. What Is YOUR Project? (In Plain English)

Your project is a **Mock Hacking & Security Training Platform** that a company runs internally.

It does 3 simple things:

1. **It sends safe, fake phishing emails to employees** to test who is careless and who is alert.
2. **It teaches them the exact second they make a mistake:** If an employee clicks the fake link and tries to enter their password, a screen immediately jumps up saying: 
   > *"Hold on! This was a security test. You didn't get hacked, but here are the 3 red flags you missed!"*
3. **It measures if your defense actually works:** It gives every department a "Human Risk Score" (like a credit score) so the CEO knows if the company is getting safer over time.

### 3. Physically, What Are You Doing On Your Laptop?

When you open **`http://localhost:3000`** in your browser, here is what you are actually looking at:

#### Step 1: The Main Dashboard (`/dashboard`)
* **What you see:** A command center showing a **Human Risk Score (28.8 / 100)**.
* **What it means:** It tells the boss: *"Our company used to have a 31% failure rate. After we started training people, only 12% fall for fake emails now, and 68% of employees click the Report button when they see something fishy."*

#### Step 2: The Attack Simulator (`/campaigns`)
* **What you physically do:** You click the blue button called **"Live Attack Sandbox"**.
* **What appears on screen:** A split-screen window. 
  - On the left side, you see a **fake email** claiming to be from the CEO asking for an urgent money transfer.
  - On the right side, you see a **live radar/log** that watches what the employee does.
* **The 2 things you can test live:**
  1. **If the employee is careless:** Click "Click Link", then type a fake password and click "Sign In". 
     - Instantly, a bright **"Teachable Moment" screen** appears! It points out: *"Look, the email was from @corp-exec-secure.com, not your real company! Look, it used fake urgency!"*
     - And in the background, your project **never saved their password** (that's the ethical rule).
  2. **If the employee is smart:** Click the green **"Report Phish"** button.
     - Confetti / Success screen! The platform records that the employee caught the attacker in 14 seconds.

#### Step 3: Proving Countermeasures Work (`/countermeasures`)
* **What you show your examiners:** 
  - You divided your employees into two test groups:
    - **Group B (No warning banner):** $26.6\%$ of people got tricked.
    - **Group A (With your warning banner & instant training):** Only $5.8\%$ got tricked.
  - **Your punchline:** *"Examiner, our warning banners and instant training reduced risk by 78%!"*

#### Step 4: The 5-Dimension Culture Survey (`/culture`)
* **What it is:** A quiz that employees take to measure how security-minded they are.
* **Why it matters:** It doesn't just ask technical questions. It measures **"Reporting Confidence"** — do employees feel safe reporting a mistake, or are they scared their boss will fire them?
* **What you physically do:** You can click the **"Take Live Assessment"** tab, pick "Finance", answer the 8 questions right in front of your professor, click Submit, and watch the score recalculate live!

#### Step 5: Department Risk Matrix (`/departments`)
* **What it shows:** 
  - **Finance** is high risk ($45.3$) because they are terrified of disobeying the CEO (Authority Bias).
  - **Marketing** is moderate risk because they click everything with an urgent deadline (Urgency Bias).
  - **Engineering** is low risk ($17.5$) because developers look at domain names.

#### Step 6: Executive Audit & Reports (`/reports`)
* **What it is:** The official certificate that the company gives to government auditors (NIST & ISO 27001) proving they train their staff properly.
* **What you physically do:** Click **"Download CSV"** to show a real spreadsheet of all the test data downloading to your laptop.
